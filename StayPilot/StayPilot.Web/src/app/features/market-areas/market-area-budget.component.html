Best fit is the most rooms a place usually sells for your budget, judged on what that typology typically costs there — not on the cheapest advert in it. One bargain T3 in Cascais does not make Cascais a place where €300,000 buys a T3. Places where the budget reaches nothing are left out rather than shown empty.

Min rooms turns the question from “where does my money buy something” into “where does it buy the T3 I actually need”, which has far fewer answers and is the one worth acting on. Stretch answers the question people really have at the edge of their means — and what would a little more get me. Places reached only by the stretch are marked stretch, never quietly mixed in with the ones that fit.

Click any row to see every typology the budget reaches there, not only the biggest. The headline answer assumes more rooms is what you want, and often it is not: the same money that reaches a small T3 also reaches a larger, cheaper-per-metre T2, and that trade is the actual decision.

The three number columns will not multiply out, and that is correct: each is its own middle value taken separately, and the middle price is not the middle size times the middle rate. Read each as “typically about this”, not as one worked example.
